How to Create a Positive Workplace Culture: 10 Practices for Leaders

Ten practical, evidence-informed ways leaders can build a positive, high-energy workplace culture, from psychological safety to recognition.

By the Positive.Energy editorial team · 8 min read · Reviewed November 2026

In this guide
  1. 10 practices
  2. Need help rolling it out?
  3. FAQ

Positive culture isn't ping-pong tables. It's how people feel when they come to work: safe to speak up, recognised, connected and clear on what matters. Those conditions are strongly linked to engagement and retention.

10 practices

1. Build psychological safety

Google's Project Aristotle found psychological safety, the shared belief that it's safe to take interpersonal risks, was the most important factor in its effective teams. Leaders build it by asking for input, admitting mistakes and thanking people for raising problems.

2. Recognise specifically and often

Specific, timely recognition ("Your summary saved the client call") beats annual awards.

3. Clarify priorities

Ambiguity drains energy. Make the top three priorities visible and say what not to do.

4. Protect focus time

Set meeting-free blocks and norms for response times.

5. Start meetings with something good

A quick "one win from this week" round sets a constructive tone without forced cheer.

6. Use strengths

Help people spend more time on work that uses their strengths. Strengths-based development is associated with higher engagement.

7. Model recovery

Leaders who take breaks, use their leave and log off signal that rest is allowed.

8. Make kindness normal

Peer shout-outs, mentoring and helping behaviours spread through teams.

9. Address the real drains

No wellbeing program fixes chronic overload or unfairness. Use burnout research (workload, control, reward, community, fairness, values) as a diagnostic.

10. Measure and iterate

Run short pulse surveys or team Energy Scores, act on one finding, and report back.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a positive workplace culture?

Psychological safety, clear priorities, regular recognition, fair workloads, opportunities to use strengths and leaders who model healthy behaviours.

How do you improve team morale quickly?

Recognise specific contributions, remove a known frustration, clarify priorities and create small moments of connection such as sharing weekly wins.
