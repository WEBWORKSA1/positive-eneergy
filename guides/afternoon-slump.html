How to Beat the Afternoon Slump (Without More Coffee)

Why energy dips after lunch and 10 practical ways to beat the 2–3 p.m. slump at work or home.

By the Positive.Energy editorial team · 6 min read · Reviewed November 2026

In this guide
  1. Before lunch
  2. At lunch
  3. During the dip
  4. If the slump is severe
  5. FAQ

If your energy drops between about 1 and 4 p.m., your body is working as designed. The circadian rhythm has a natural dip in alertness in the early afternoon, and a heavy lunch or short night makes it deeper. You can't delete the dip, but you can flatten it.

Before lunch

  • Get morning light. A well-set body clock tends to give you a more stable day.
  • Front-load deep work. Do demanding thinking in the morning and schedule admin for the dip.

At lunch

  • Balance the plate. Protein, vegetables and fibre with your carbs, rather than a big refined-carb meal.
  • Eat away from the desk. A real break helps you come back fresher.
  • Walk after eating. Ten minutes of walking after a meal can help moderate the blood-sugar rise that often precedes a crash.

During the dip

  • Go outside. Daylight and fresh air are fast pick-me-ups.
  • Stand and move. Take calls standing, or do ten squats.
  • Energizing breath. A minute of quicker, rhythmic breathing (2 seconds in, 2 out) can feel activating. Stop if you get light-headed. Try the "energize" setting on our breathing pacer.
  • Hydrate. Afternoon fatigue is sometimes thirst in disguise.
  • Short nap (if you can). 10–20 minutes, before mid-afternoon, tends to refresh without grogginess.
  • Switch task type. Move from screen work to a conversation, a walk-and-talk or a hands-on task.

If the slump is severe

A daily crash that makes it hard to function can point to poor sleep, sleep apnoea, blood-sugar issues or other health conditions. Talk to a doctor if it's persistent.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I get tired at 3pm?

A natural circadian dip in alertness occurs in the early afternoon, often deepened by a large lunch, short sleep, dehydration or long periods of sitting.

What should I eat to avoid an afternoon crash?

Meals that combine protein, fibre and healthy fats with carbohydrates tend to give steadier energy than sugary or refined-carb meals.
