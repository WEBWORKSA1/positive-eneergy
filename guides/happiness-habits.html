10 Daily Habits of Happy People (Backed by Research)

Ten everyday habits that research links to greater happiness and life satisfaction, and how to build them one at a time.

Happiness is partly genetic and partly circumstance, but a meaningful share appears to come from what we do every day. These ten habits come up again and again in wellbeing research.

1. They invest in relationships

The Harvard Study of Adult Development, running since 1938, found warm relationships to be among the strongest predictors of happiness and health in later life. Robert Waldinger's talk on it is in our video library.

2. They express gratitude

Noticing and voicing appreciation is consistently linked to higher wellbeing. See our gratitude guide.

3. They move their bodies

Regular physical activity is linked to better mood and lower risk of depression. Any movement counts.

4. They spend time in nature

Studies have associated spending around two hours a week in nature with better reported health and wellbeing.

5. They're kind

Spending money or time on others, known as prosocial behaviour, reliably lifts the giver's mood in experiments.

6. They savour

Happy people linger on good moments: sharing them, noticing details, reminiscing. Savouring stretches positive emotion.

7. They pursue meaningful goals

Progress on goals that fit your values, rather than goals driven by approval, is more satisfying.

8. They sleep well

Sleep loss amplifies negative emotions and dampens positive ones. Sleep is a happiness habit.

9. They buy experiences and time

Research suggests spending on experiences, and on buying back time from chores, tends to bring more happiness than spending on things.

10. They practise acceptance

Happy people still feel sadness and anger. They accept emotions instead of fighting them, which helps them pass sooner.

Build them one at a time

Choose one habit for two weeks. Attach it to an existing routine. The 30-day positivity challenge rotates through most of these, one small action a day.

Frequently asked questions

What habits make people happiest?

Research highlights strong relationships, gratitude, physical activity, kindness, time in nature, good sleep and pursuing meaningful goals.

Can you become happier?

Studies suggest intentional activities like gratitude, kindness and savouring can raise happiness levels for many people.
