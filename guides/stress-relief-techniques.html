12 Fast Stress-Relief Techniques You Can Use Anywhere

Twelve quick, evidence-informed ways to calm down in minutes, from the physiological sigh to grounding, plus a plan for long-term stress resilience.

By the Positive.Energy editorial team · 8 min read · Reviewed November 2026

In this guide
  1. In under a minute
  2. In 2–5 minutes
  3. In 10–20 minutes
  4. Building long-term stress resilience
  5. FAQ

Stress isn't the enemy. A healthy stress response helps you rise to a challenge. The problem is staying switched on for too long. These twelve techniques help you come back down fast, and most take under five minutes.

In under a minute

1. The physiological sigh

Inhale through your nose, add a second short inhale on top, then exhale slowly through your mouth. Repeat two or three times. Research from Stanford found that cyclic sighing done for five minutes a day improved mood and reduced breathing rate more than mindfulness meditation in their sample.

2. Name it to tame it

Putting a feeling into words ("I'm anxious about this meeting") is linked to lower emotional intensity. Say it quietly or write it down.

3. Drop your shoulders

Stress lives in the body. Unclench your jaw, lower your shoulders, soften your hands. Releasing physical tension sends a safety signal back to the brain.

In 2–5 minutes

4. Box breathing

Inhale 4, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4. Used widely for performance under pressure. Our breathing pacer guides the timing.

5. 5-4-3-2-1 grounding

Name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell and 1 you taste. It pulls attention out of worry and into the present.

6. Cold water on your face or wrists

A splash of cool water can help you feel calmer quickly. It's a simple sensory reset.

7. A brisk walk

Movement uses up the stress response the way it was designed to be used. Even five minutes helps.

8. Shake it out

Shake your hands, arms and legs for 30–60 seconds. It feels silly and works surprisingly well for releasing nervous energy before a big moment.

In 10–20 minutes

9. Progressive muscle relaxation

Tense each muscle group for five seconds, then release, moving from your feet to your face. It's a well-established relaxation technique.

10. Write the worry, then the next step

Spend ten minutes writing everything on your mind. Then circle one item you can act on and write the next tiny step.

11. Call a friend

Social support is a powerful stress buffer. Talking through what's going on with someone you trust often shrinks the problem.

12. Reframe stress as fuel

Kelly McGonigal's popular talk (in our video library) discusses research suggesting that seeing your stress response as helpful, rather than harmful, may change how you experience it. Try saying: "My body is getting ready to meet this."

Building long-term stress resilience

Quick techniques handle the moment. Resilience comes from the basics: regular sleep, movement, connection, time in nature and a sense of control over your days. If stress is constant, look at the source. Workload, boundaries or a coach can help with that. See how to build resilience.

If stress comes with panic attacks, persistent low mood, or thoughts of harming yourself, please reach out for professional help. See support lines.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest way to calm down?

A few rounds of slow breathing with a long exhale, such as the physiological sigh (double inhale, long exhale), is one of the quickest ways to settle your body within a minute.

How can I reduce stress at work quickly?

Use box breathing or a short walk between tasks, name what you're feeling, and break the stressful task into one concrete next step.
