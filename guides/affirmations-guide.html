Do Affirmations Work? What the Research Says (and How to Use Them)

The honest science on positive affirmations, why some backfire, and how to write affirmations that actually help.

By the Positive.Energy editorial team · 6 min read · Reviewed November 2026

In this guide

  2. How to write affirmations that work
  3. A simple daily practice
  4. The values exercise (10 minutes)
  5. FAQ

Affirmations have a mixed reputation, and the research explains why. Used one way they can help. Used another, they can backfire.

What the research says

Self-affirmation theory (Claude Steele) studies something specific: reflecting on your core values. In many experiments, writing about an important value before a stressful task reduced defensiveness and helped people perform under pressure. That's the best-supported form of "affirmation."

By contrast, a well-known 2009 study by Joanne Wood and colleagues found that repeating a broad statement like "I am a lovable person" made people with low self-esteem feel worse, possibly because the statement clashed with their beliefs.

How to write affirmations that work

  • Make them believable. "I'm learning to speak up in meetings" beats "I am a fearless leader."
  • Anchor them in values. "I show up for the people I love" connects to what matters.
  • Use process words. "I am learning," "I am practising," "I choose to…"
  • Pair them with action. Follow the affirmation with one tiny behaviour that proves it.
  • Try second or third person. Some research on "distanced self-talk" suggests addressing yourself by name ("You've got this, Sam") can help regulate emotion.

A simple daily practice

  1. Pick one theme for the week (confidence, calm, energy…).
  2. Choose or write one believable affirmation. Our affirmation generator has six themes.
  3. Say it once in the morning, then do one action that matches it.
  4. At night, note any evidence you lived it.

The values exercise (10 minutes)

Before a stressful event, choose your most important value and write for ten minutes about why it matters and a time you lived it. This is the version of affirmation with the strongest research behind it.

Frequently asked questions

Do positive affirmations really work?

Value-based self-affirmation has good research support for reducing defensiveness and stress. Broad, unbelievable statements may backfire for people with low self-esteem, so believable, specific affirmations tied to action work better.

How many times a day should I say affirmations?

Quality beats quantity. Once or twice a day, paired with a small action, is more effective than mindless repetition.
