Burnout: Signs, Causes and a Practical Recovery Plan

How to recognise burnout, what really causes it, and a step-by-step recovery plan for individuals and managers.

By the Positive.Energy editorial team · 9 min read · Reviewed November 2026

In this guide
  1. The three signs
  2. The six common causes
  3. A recovery plan
  4. For managers
  5. FAQ

The World Health Organization's ICD-11 describes burnout as an occupational phenomenon resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed. It's not a personal failing. It's usually a mismatch between you and your work conditions.

The three signs

  • Exhaustion: feeling depleted, with rest no longer refilling the tank.
  • Cynicism or distance: feeling detached, negative or numb about your work.
  • Reduced efficacy: feeling you're not getting anything done or doing it well.

The six common causes

Christina Maslach and Michael Leiter's research highlights six areas where mismatches drive burnout: workload, control, reward, community, fairness and values. Identifying which ones apply is the first step to fixing the right thing.

A recovery plan

Phase 1: Stop the bleeding (weeks 1–2)

  • Talk to a doctor. Burnout overlaps with depression and other conditions that deserve proper assessment.
  • Cut or delegate anything non-essential. Say no to new commitments.
  • Protect sleep and take any leave you can.

Phase 2: Restore (weeks 3–8)

  • Rebuild basics: regular sleep, daily movement, time outdoors, meals.
  • Reconnect with people and activities that once energised you.
  • Use short daily practices: breathing, three good things, a walk.

Phase 3: Redesign (ongoing)

  • Look at the six areas. Which mismatch can you change, negotiate or leave?
  • Set boundaries around hours and availability.
  • Consider a coach to plan changes, or a therapist if mood remains low.

For managers

Burnout is largely an organisational problem. Clarify priorities, match workload to capacity, give people control over how they work, recognise effort and model taking breaks. Our workplace resilience programs help teams do this systematically.

Frequently asked questions

How long does burnout recovery take?

It varies widely, from weeks to many months, depending on severity and whether the underlying work conditions change.

Is burnout a medical diagnosis?

The WHO classifies burnout as an occupational phenomenon, not a medical condition, but its symptoms overlap with conditions like depression, so a medical check is wise.
